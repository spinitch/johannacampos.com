How I Can Help

Dissect + Diagnose is for teams who need clarity before they commit further resources. I developed this package after being brought onto the commercialization effort for an AI clinical platform — the team had built something technically strong, but nobody had stopped to ask who it was actually for. That gap between “it works” and “it’s ready” is what this audit is built to find.

Who it’s for

Founders and teams ready to move to the next phase and want an outside, senior perspective to make sure they’re building on solid ground, as well as those who sense something’s off but can’t quite pinpoint what.

What you get

The exact mix depends on where you are and what you’re trying to learn, but the work generally moves through three stages:

  • Research:
    Interviews with the right stakeholders and users, journey mapping, and a blueprint of how the experience actually works across touchpoints and environments.
  • Diagnosis:
    A clear-eyed evaluation of what’s working and what isn’t, with findings named and ranked by impact.
  • Recommendations:
    Fixes you can act on immediately, and the bigger strategic moves worth considering, delivered as a written report with an optional debrief.

Case Study

For an AI clinical platform preparing to commercialize, I was brought in to assess its commercial viability. The team had already done strong work tuning the model for accuracy, working with talented data scientists to get the numbers right. What hadn’t been tested was direction.

Physician interviews told a different story than the model metrics did. The tool didn’t have a clear point of view on who it was for or what problem it solved first. People used it anyway, mostly because it was there and it was free, but the frustration was real and consistent across roles. My recommendation was to treat product strategy and user experience as the next phase of the work, not an afterthought to a technically strong model.

I delivered a prioritized roadmap alongside the audit findings, with clear next steps the team could act on immediately versus plan for later. We kept regular check-ins through the engagement so nothing sat in a report unread — recommendations got discussed and adjusted as the team’s own thinking evolved.

Design + Deliver is for teams who have proven the concept and now need it to hold up in front of real users, investors, or partners. I developed this package out of my own experience building Lakatta from an idea into a working product — knowing firsthand what it takes to go from “this could work” to something people actually trust and use.

Who it’s for

Teams with a working product who need senior design execution, not just direction, to close the gap between functional and great.

What you get

The exact mix depends on the product and where it is in its lifecycle, but the work generally moves through three stages:

  • Define:
    Understanding the users, the constraints, and what good looks like for this product, translated into a direction the whole team can build against.
  • Design:
    Wireframes, prototypes, and high-fidelity UI, tested and refined with real users along the way rather than delivered cold.
  • Deliver:
    A design system and production-ready files, handed off with the specs and rationale your engineering team needs to build it right, with support through implementation.
  • Add-on (optional) — a working prototype:
    Instead of static files, I build the design out into a working, clickable prototype using AI-native tools, the same approach behind Lakatta. Useful when you need something real to test with users or show investors, not just a design to hand off.

Case Study

I’ve watched the same scene play out on every family trip: one person holds all the information, and everyone else is texting them for the Airbnb code, the flight number, which terminal to go to. I’ve stood in an airport digging through my own inbox trying to find a terminal number while stressed and short on time. There had to be a better way to handle information that’s already sitting in your email.

I looked at what existed. TripIt, Wanderlog, they solve the aggregation problem, but not the simplicity problem. I thought I could build something better, something simpler. So Lakatta pulls your confirmation emails into one itinerary that shows you only what you need for right now, with the rest of the trip a tap away. Weather’s built in. Shared trips and split expenses mean nobody’s re-sending hotel details in a group chat for the fifth time.
